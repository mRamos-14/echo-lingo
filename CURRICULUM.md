# Echo Lingo — curriculum

How Echo Lingo teaches Korean, Japanese and Mandarin Chinese to someone starting from zero.
Explanations are in plain English. Every new word or pattern is heard before it is read.

---

## How every lesson works

1. **Hear it first.** A new word plays 2–3 times before you see any writing.
2. **Then see it.** The script appears, with a romanized helper (fades out as you level up).
3. **Then the meaning**, plus a "how it works" card if there's a new rule.
4. **Pattern card.** Grammar is taught as a fill-in formula, e.g. `[person] 는 [thing] 을 [action]`.
5. **Drills.** Listen → meaning, listen → writing, sound pairs, build the sentence.
6. **Mix with old material.** About 30% of every lesson is review, chosen by spaced repetition.

One new idea per lesson. Nothing is used before it has been taught.

### Romanization helper ("training wheels")

| Tier | Helper shown |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Always shown |
| 2 | Shown, tap to hide |
| 3 | Hidden, tap to reveal |
| 4+ | Off (Chinese keeps pinyin on new words only) |

## The big picture: three languages, two families

- **Korean and Japanese are grammar twins.** Both put the action last (who · what · do) and use small
  particle tags after words (는/은 ≈ は topic, 을/를 ≈ を object, 에 ≈ に place/time). Learning one makes the other easier.
- **Chinese is grammar-simple but sound-hard.** Word order is like English (who · do · what), verbs never change
  form, there are no particles marking roles, but every syllable has a tone that changes its meaning.
- **Japanese and Chinese share characters.** Japanese kanji came from Chinese hanzi, so many characters overlap.
- **Korean shares old Chinese roots.** A big part of Korean vocabulary is borrowed from Chinese.

### Cousin words (a feature for learning all three)

When a word has relatives in the other two languages, the lesson shows them together:

| Meaning | Korean | Japanese | Chinese |
|---|---|---|---|
| student | 학생 haksaeng | 学生 gakusei | 学生 xuésheng |
| school | 학교 hakgyo | 学校 gakkō | 学校 xuéxiào |
| library | 도서관 doseogwan | 図書館 toshokan | 图书馆 túshūguǎn |
| one, two, three | 일, 이, 삼 il, i, sam | いち, に, さん ichi, ni, san | 一, 二, 三 yī, èr, sān |

## Korean 🇰🇷

**Writing:** Hangul, an alphabet of 24 basic letters (14 consonants, 10 vowels) stacked into syllable blocks:
`ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ = 한`. Fully learnable in a few days.
**Hard parts:** the three-way consonant contrast (ㄱ plain / ㅋ breathy / ㄲ tense), final-consonant sound changes,
two number systems, and speech levels (how polite you are changes the verb ending).

### Tier 0 — Sounds (Hangul)
1. How a block is built; the 5 shape-of-the-mouth consonants (ㄱ ㄴ ㅁ ㅅ ㅇ) and silent ㅇ
2. Basic vowels ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ ㅣ ㅐ ㅔ
3. Basic consonants ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅈ ㅎ
4. Breathy ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ vs tense ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ (sound-pair drills: 달 / 탈 / 딸)
5. Y-vowels ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ ㅒ ㅖ and w-vowels ㅘ ㅝ ㅙ ㅞ ㅚ ㅟ ㅢ
6. Final consonants (받침) and the 7 final sounds
7. Linking: how 음악 sounds like 으막 · Tier 0 review: read any Hangul word aloud

### Tier 1 — Kindergarten (~150 words)
Greetings and thanks · yes/no · "This is ~" (이에요/예요) · Sino numbers 일–십 · native numbers 하나–열 ·
family · food · colors · body · animals · classroom objects

### Tier 2 — Lower elementary (≈ TOPIK 1)
Polite present tense -아요/-어요 · particles 은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 에, 에서 · 있어요/없어요 ·
negation 안 · questions · time and days · money and shopping

### Tier 3 — Upper elementary (≈ TOPIK 2)
Past -았/었어요 · future -(으)ㄹ 거예요 · want -고 싶어요 · can -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 · counters (개, 명, 잔) ·
directions · daily routine · weather

### Tier 4 — Middle school (≈ TOPIK 3)
Linking: -고, -아서/어서, -지만, -(으)니까 · formal -습니다 · casual 반말 · plans and reasons ·
comparisons · phone calls

### Tier 5 — High school (≈ TOPIK 4)
-는데 · quoting what someone said · -(으)면서 · passive and causative basics · telling stories ·
opinions · faster natural speech

### Tier 6 — College (≈ TOPIK 5–6)
Honorifics (-시-, 드리다, 께서) · written style -다 · four-character idioms (사자성어) · news and lectures ·
debate language

## Japanese 🇯🇵

**Writing:** three scripts used together. Hiragana (46, native words and grammar), katakana (46, foreign
words), and kanji (Chinese characters for meaning). One sentence can mix all three.
**Hard parts:** kanji (each has several readings), politeness forms, and the many verb forms. Pronunciation is
the easiest of the three: 5 vowels, and each beat (mora) gets equal time.

### Tier 0 — Sounds (kana)
1. The 3 scripts and why; the 5 vowels あ い う え お
2. Hiragana か さ た な rows
3. Hiragana は ま や ら わ rows + を ん
4. Voiced marks: が ざ だ ば ぱ
5. Small ゃ ゅ ょ (きゃ), small っ (double consonant), long vowels (おばさん aunt vs おばあさん grandma)
6. Katakana (same sounds, new shapes) + the long mark ー
7. Pitch: はし (chopsticks) vs はし (bridge) · Tier 0 review: read any kana word aloud

### Tier 1 — Kindergarten (~150 words)
Greetings and thanks · "This is ~" (これは ~です) · numbers いち–じゅう · family · food · colors · body ·
animals · first 20 picture-like kanji (人 日 月 山 川 口 木 …)

### Tier 2 — Lower elementary (≈ JLPT N5)
Polite verbs -ます / -ません · particles は, が, を, に, で, へ, と · い- and な-adjectives · ある/いる (there is) ·
questions with か · time and days · shopping · ~80 more kanji

### Tier 3 — Upper elementary (≈ N5–N4)
Past -ました · the て-form (requests -てください, ongoing -ている) · want -たい · counters (つ, 人, 枚, 本) ·
directions · daily routine · weather

### Tier 4 — Middle school (≈ N4–N3)
Plain/casual form · reasons から / ので · even if -ても · potential (can) · "I think" -と思う ·
giving and receiving (あげる / くれる / もらう)

### Tier 5 — High school (≈ N3–N2)
Conditionals -ば / -たら / -と · passive · causative · seems/looks -そう / -よう / -らしい · stories · opinions ·
natural-speed conversation

### Tier 6 — College (≈ N2–N1)
Keigo: respectful and humble speech · written style である · four-character idioms (四字熟語) ·
news and lectures · nuance between similar grammar

## Mandarin Chinese 🇨🇳

**Writing:** characters (汉字), each one a syllable with a meaning, built from reusable parts (radicals).
Pinyin is a Latin-letter spelling for the sounds, used for learning and typing.
**Hard parts:** tones (mā mom / má hemp / mǎ horse / mà scold) and the number of characters. Grammar is the
easiest of the three: no verb conjugation, no plural endings, word order close to English.

### Tier 0 — Sounds (pinyin + tones)
1. A syllable = start sound + end sound + tone
2. The 4 tones + neutral tone (heavy sound-pair drilling)
3. Easy starts: b p m f d t n l g k h
4. Tricky starts: j q x · zh ch sh r · z c s
5. End sounds, including ü (as in 女 nǚ)
6. Tone changes: 3+3 → 2+3 (你好 ní hǎo), 不 bù → bú, 一 yī changes
7. How characters are built: strokes and radicals (口 人 女 木 水 火) · Tier 0 review

### Tier 1 — Kindergarten (~150 words)
Greetings and thanks · numbers 一–十 and how 11–99 build logically · family · food · colors · body · animals ·
"is" 是 · yes/no questions with 吗 · measure word 个

### Tier 2 — Lower elementary (≈ HSK 1)
有 (have) · 在 (at) · 不 vs 没 · more measure words (本, 杯, 只) · possession 的 · word order: time → place → action
(我今天在家吃饭) · questions with 什么, 哪儿, 谁 · money and shopping

### Tier 3 — Upper elementary (≈ HSK 2)
Done 了 · experienced 过 · ongoing 在/正在 · want/can 想, 要, 会, 能 · comparisons with 比 · directions ·
daily routine · weather

### Tier 4 — Middle school (≈ HSK 3)
把 sentences · result endings (看完 finished reading, 听懂 understood by listening) · because/so 因为…所以 ·
although/but 虽然…但是 · direction endings (上来, 出去)

### Tier 5 — High school (≈ HSK 4)
Passive 被 · even 连…都 · emphasis 是…的 · more complements · stories · opinions · natural-speed conversation

### Tier 6 — College (≈ HSK 5–6)
Four-character idioms (成语) · formal written Chinese (书面语) · news and lectures · debate language

---

## Unit shape (all languages, all tiers)

- 4–6 lessons per unit, 3–5 minutes each, ~6–10 new items per lesson
- Unit review at the end: a mixed listening challenge; must pass to unlock the next unit
- Tier exam at the end of each tier
- Dialogues start in Tier 1 (2 lines) and grow to full conversations by Tier 4
